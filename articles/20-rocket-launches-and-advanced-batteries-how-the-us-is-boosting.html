Rocket Launches and Advanced Batteries: How the US is Boosting Local Tech

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 6d ago

The US government is ramping up support for rocket launches and domestic batteries to secure the nation's high-tech future. A new government space policy aims to prepare for a massive boom in rocket flights. The goal is to support more than 1,000 launches and landings every single year by 2030. To handle this, officials want to upgrade spaceports, which are essentially airports for rockets. These sites, like the famous Cape Canaveral in Florida, will get infrastructure upgrades to handle intense demand from both private space companies and the military.

At the same time, the government is focusing on how we power our high-tech gear. The energy department is handing out $500 million in grants to American battery startups. Many of these young companies struggled after electric vehicle incentives were cut. Fortunately, they have found a vital new customer in the military. The defense sector needs lightweight, US-made batteries for drones, radios, and fighter jets to avoid depending on foreign nations.

Some of these startups are getting massive financial boosts. For example, a company named Lilac Solutions got $100 million to produce lithium, a key metal used in batteries, in Utah by 2028. Another startup called Nth Cycle secured $100 million to recycle old batteries into fresh materials.

Why does this matter to you? These moves mean the technology we rely on every day, from satellite internet to advanced electronics, will be built closer to home. It keeps the local tech industry strong and protects essential services from global supply chain issues.

Whether it is launching rockets or building better batteries, the government is clearly prioritizing domestic tech. The future of both space travel and energy is moving fast, and these plans aim to keep the US at the front of the pack.
