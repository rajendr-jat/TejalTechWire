AI Wins a Court Battle While Bad Weather Delays a Mission to the Moon

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 2d ago

This week, big tech legal battles and wild weather are changing the future of artificial intelligence and space travel. First, a US judge made a huge decision about how AI models are built. Tech company Anthropic was ordered to pay a $1.5 billion fine because they trained their AI using pirated books. However, the judge actually ruled that training AI on copyrighted books is legal, comparing it to a human reading books to learn how to write.

This matters to you because it shapes how future AI tools will behave and what they can read. The decision comes down to "fair use," which is a legal rule that lets people use copyrighted material without permission if they create something brand new. Since our main copyright laws were written back in 1976, judges are working hard to apply old rules to new tech. Basically, if an AI is built to compete directly with the original author, courts will stop it. But if the AI is just learning to build something different, judges say it is okay.

Meanwhile, far away from the courtroom, China had to pause its massive mission to the moon. They delayed their Chang'e 7 spacecraft launch, which was supposed to happen this week, and pushed it back. This mission includes a lunar orbiter, a lander, a rover, and a small jumping probe. Bad weather from Typhoon Narra is the main reason for the delay. The storm shut down the launch window, which is the short timeframe when weather and orbits are perfect for a safe liftoff.

Because of the bad weather, the next safe launch window might not open for a long time, potentially pushing the mission as far back as 2027. Whether it is writing code or flying to the stars, tech progress takes patience and careful planning. We will keep you updated as these stories unfold.
