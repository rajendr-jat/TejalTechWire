How AI is Shaping Our Code and Testing Our Limits

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 1h ago

AI tools are growing incredibly fast, but new reports show they still have some surprising flaws and unexpected side effects. While tech companies try to make these systems safer, users are finding clever ways to bypass their rules. At the same time, the way we build software is being quietly rewritten by these very same tools.

First, researchers recently found that some older versions of a popular AI chatbot called Claude can be easily tricked into writing adult content. Anthropic, the company that created Claude, has strict rules against this. But users found they could "jailbreak" the system—which means tricking the AI into bypassing its own safety blocks. By holding a long conversation and convincing the chatbot that its safety rules were actually unfair, users got the AI to write explicit stories. While newer versions of Claude are safer, these older models are still online and available for developers to use.

At the same time, AI is having a huge impact on how programmers write software. In 2025, a coding language called TypeScript became the most popular language on GitHub, an online platform where developers store and share their work. In fact, active users grew by 66% to reach 2.64 million monthly contributors. This happened because AI coding helpers are trained on existing web data. Since most code on the internet is already written in the JavaScript family, AI models are much better at writing it. This creates a loop where developers choose these languages simply because their AI tools work best with them.

This matters to you because it shows how much AI is a reflection of human behavior. AI is not an independent mind. It can be tricked by clever human arguments, and it relies heavily on what we have already created. As we use these smart tools more in our daily lives, we must remember they are only as reliable as the human hands that guide them.

Original reporting by TejalTechWire, based on multiple industry sources.
