US Regulators Crack Down on TikTok and New Flying Drone

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 0m ago

The US government is cracking down on popular technology, hitting TikTok with a massive fine and blocking a new flying camera drone from being sold. TikTok has agreed to pay $400 million to settle a 2024 lawsuit over children's privacy. US officials accused the app of breaking a law called COPPA, which protects the online data of kids. The government said TikTok let children under 13 make accounts and kept their personal information even when parents asked to delete it. TikTok has since changed its settings to protect young users and restructured its US business. It will pay $300 million of the fine immediately, and the remaining $100 million later.

Meanwhile, US gadget fans are missing out on a unique new device called the HoverAir Versa. This product is a modular drone, which means it is a basic camera that snaps into flying wings to become a drone. Just 3 days after it launched on a crowdfunding website, the company had to stop taking orders from US customers. The Federal Communications Commission, or FCC—the government group that approves wireless tech—seems to have canceled the device's approval.

The Chinese company that makes the drone tried to get around strict US drone rules by registering the device as a simple camera. However, the FCC blocks all Chinese drones and their main parts. Because the camera needs the wings to fly, the agency likely decided the loophole did not work. Now, US buyers cannot get the flight kit, and they might not even get the camera.

This matters to you because it shows how much governments are focusing on tech security and privacy. While these rules keep your personal data safe, they can also stop cool new gadgets from reaching your living room. We will likely see many more tech companies struggle to launch products as these rules get even tighter.
