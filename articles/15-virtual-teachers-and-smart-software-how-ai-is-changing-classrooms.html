Virtual Teachers and Smart Software: How AI is Changing Classrooms and the Internet

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 7d ago

Two major new developments show how artificial intelligence is quickly moving into our daily lives, from university classrooms to the plumbing of the internet.

First, Harvard Business School is now using digital clones of its instructors to teach students. The school has an 8-week online training program for new business owners called HBS Foundry, which costs $699. While human teachers still run weekly live sessions, AI avatars give students instant feedback on their business pitches. A startup called HeyGen created these digital clones. One reporter tried pitching an idea to a virtual version of a real investor. The avatar offered a slightly frozen smile, but the real investor said his students love the digital copy.

Meanwhile, behind the scenes, payment giant Stripe is buying a company called OpenRouter. OpenRouter is a tool that helps software developers connect to more than 400 different AI models from 80 providers. An AI model is the brain behind a smart chatbot. Instead of relying on just one AI, OpenRouter acts like a smart traffic controller. It automatically sends a user's request to the fastest, cheapest, or most reliable AI model available at that exact second. This prevents website crashes and saves companies money.

Why does this matter to you? It shows that AI is no longer just a fun toy for writing poems or making silly images. Instead, it is becoming a practical tool that helps people learn new skills and keeps the websites you use every day running smoothly. Whether you are practicing a business speech in front of a digital teacher or just shopping online, smart computer systems are quietly working in the background to make things faster and cheaper.

As these technologies grow, we will likely see even more virtual helpers and smarter background systems. It is an exciting look at how the future of work and learning is taking shape.
