Meta Faces Court Battles Over Teen Safety While US Government Raises Tech Visa Fees

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 3d ago

Big tech companies are facing a double headache this week as Meta fights a major lawsuit over teen safety and the US government raises hiring fees.

In a recent court trial, Instagram boss Adam Mosseri testified about how his app affects young people. He claimed he did not know his own staff had hid worrying safety data from him. Internal company reports from 2023 showed that teenagers were seeing high amounts of content about eating disorders and self-harm. In fact, teens saw this harmful content at much higher rates than adults. The court also heard that Instagram's "take a break" feature, which reminds kids to stop scrolling, was only used by 1.8 percent of users after it launched in 2021. A judge even questioned why design leaders did not know the basic safety numbers for their own app.

This matters to you because it shows how social media companies handle the mental health of young users. If you or your children use these apps, it reveals how difficult it is to get teens to log off, even with safety tools in place.

At the same time, the US government is making it much harder for tech giants to hire staff. The government is introducing a massive fee of over $103,265 for H-1B visas. This visa is a special permit that allows US companies to temporarily hire highly skilled workers from other countries. Tech giants like Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft use these visas the most. This huge cost will not just affect tech. It could also hurt rural hospitals and schools that rely on skilled foreign workers. Business leaders worry that these fees will force companies to set up offices and hire people outside of the US instead.

Between pressure over child safety and rising costs to hire top talent, tech giants are facing a very challenging time. We will have to wait and see how these big changes affect the gadgets and apps we use every day.
