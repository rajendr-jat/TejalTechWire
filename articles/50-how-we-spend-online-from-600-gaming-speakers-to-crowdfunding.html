How We Spend Online: From $600 Gaming Speakers to Crowdfunding Lifelines

TO
TejalTechWire Original · 10d ago

ASUS has launched its first-ever gaming soundbar for $600, while fundraising site GoFundMe has grown into a major digital safety net. These two developments show the very different ways we use technology today. Some of us are buying high-end gear for entertainment. Others are using digital platforms just to get by. Both show how much we rely on the internet in our daily lives.

The new ASUS speaker is called the ROG Gjallar. It costs $600 and is built for people who play online games with friends. It comes with a special control hub and built-in microphones. These microphones block out background noise so you can talk to teammates clearly without wearing a headset. The soundbar also uses Dolby Atmos. This is a special audio technology that makes three-dimensional sound bounce around your room. It also includes a wireless subwoofer, which is a separate speaker that produces deep bass sounds.

Meanwhile, other tech tools are helping people survive hard times. Tim Cadogan, the chief executive of GoFundMe, recently discussed how his website became a crucial backup plan starting in 2020. GoFundMe is a for-profit company that operates in 20 countries. Even though it is a business, it has become a primary way for people to pay for medical bills, fund local projects, or get help after disasters. It acts as a safety net when people have nowhere else to turn.

Why does this matter to you? It shows that technology is about much more than just fancy gadgets. The internet is now where we go to find joy, build communities, and seek help during emergencies. Whether you want to upgrade your gaming setup or support someone in need, technology connects us all. It is a powerful reminder of how digital tools shape our modern world.
