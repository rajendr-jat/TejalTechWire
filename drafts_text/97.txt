TITLE: Instagram's latest feature lets you put tagged posts on your main profile grid

CONTENT:
Instagram is rolling out a helpful update for your profile page. Instagram's latest feature lets you take photos and videos that your friends tagged you in and display them directly on your main profile grid. 

Before this change, any photo you were tagged in only lived in a separate, hidden tab on your profile. Now, you do not have to awkwardly repost the same photo just to show it off to your main followers.

HEADING: How It Works

You can easily add a tagged photo to your grid right from the notification you get in your direct messages when someone tags you. You can also do this directly from the post itself or by looking through your tagged photos tab. If you change your mind later, you can take the post off your main grid without deleting the original photo or removing your tag.

HEADING: Perfect for Creators and Friends

This tool is great for everyday users who want to share a fun group photo that a friend posted first. It is also highly useful for online creators and businesses. Creators can now showcase collaborative projects and brand campaigns on their main pages without having to upload duplicate files.

HEADING: More Control Over Your Profile

This update is part of a larger effort to give people more freedom over how their social media pages look. Instagram recently introduced a tool in 2024 that lets users rearrange the order of the photos on their grids. They also added new video editing tools and a feature to swap out the music on your posts at any time.

HEADING: Quick Questions Answered

Q: Does adding a tagged post copy the photo to my account?

A: No, it does not duplicate the post or save a new version to your account, so the original post remains exactly where it was.

Q: Can I remove a tagged post from my grid later?

A: Yes, you can easily remove the post from your main grid at any time, and it will still show up in your tagged photos tab.

Q: How do I add a tagged post to my profile?

A: You can add it directly from your direct message notifications, from the post itself, or from your tagged photos tab.
