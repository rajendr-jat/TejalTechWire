TITLE: Fambot Introduces An AI Chief Of Staff To Help Busy Families Organize Their Lives

CONTENT:
Artificial intelligence is moving fast, and now it wants to help you manage your household chores. A new startup called Fambot introduces an AI chief of staff designed specifically to help busy families keep up with school events, calendars, and emails. Instead of parents spending hours sorting through messages, this smart virtual assistant does the heavy lifting for them.

At the same time, tech giant Google is working behind the scenes to make virtual assistants even smarter. Researchers from Google Cloud AI and top universities have built a new tool called EnvHarness. This software acts as a special training layer that helps AI bots learn faster by creating more adaptive challenges for them, rather than using old, unchanging test programs.

HEADING: How Fambot Helps Parents Unplug

Fambot was created by former engineers and managers from Uber and Instagram who were tired of the constant flood of family emails, school flyers, and WhatsApp chats. To use the tool, you simply connect your email, calendar, and group chats. The bot then creates a clean, daily to-do list and warns you about upcoming events. You can talk to it through a web browser, a smartphone app, or even over simple text messages. The startup recently raised $3.5 million in early funding and has already been tested by more than 1,000 households, ranging from single-parent homes to large families. The creators promise that your private family data is never used to train their AI models.

HEADING: Google Makes AI Training Smarter

While Fambot tackles real-world family schedules, Google is focused on the brains of these AI systems. Normally, AI agents learn how to do things by practicing inside static, frozen virtual environments. This means the tests never change, even as the AI gets better at solving them. Google's new EnvHarness tool changes this by wrapping those old training programs in a programmable layer. This layer automatically changes the rules and makes the tasks harder when the AI gets too comfortable, helping the virtual assistant learn up to 9.0 points better than before.

HEADING: Quick Questions Answered

Q: How much does Fambot cost to use?

A: The startup has not announced public pricing details yet, but they have raised $3.5 million in early funding to build and expand the service.

Q: Can I use Google EnvHarness for my own projects?

A: Yes, Google has released EnvHarness as free, open-source software that developers can download and run on their own systems.

Q: Does Fambot share my family's private messages?

A: No, the creators of Fambot state that none of their AI models are allowed to train on or share user data.
