TITLE: Three hikers rescued after using Google Gemini to plan dangerous mountain trip

CONTENT:
Three young hikers rescued from a cold California mountain learned the hard way that artificial intelligence is not always a reliable travel guide. The group used Google Gemini, which is Google's popular AI chatbot helper, to plan their journey up Mount Shasta. 

However, the advice they received left them stranded in the dark with very little food and water. This scary incident shows why relying only on computer programs for outdoor safety can be highly dangerous.

HEADING: What Went Wrong on the Mountain

The hikers started their climb at 3 am but made a major mistake by continuing to the top long after they should have turned back. Safety rules state that hikers should head back down by 12 pm if they have not reached the peak, but this group did not arrive until 7 pm. They quickly got lost in the dark while trying to walk down, forcing them to spend a freezing night in a canyon before rescue teams saved them the next morning.

HEADING: Bad Advice from the AI Chatbot

Local rescue officials pointed out that the Google Gemini app told the group to bring much less food and water than they actually needed. This bad advice became a major safety issue when their planned 8 hour hike turned into a multi-day emergency. Local authorities are now urging everyone to contact real human park rangers for the most accurate safety details instead of trusting AI.

HEADING: Quick Questions Answered

Q: Where did the rescue take place?
A: The rescue happened on Mount Shasta, which is a popular mountain located in northern California.

Q: What bad advice did the AI give the hikers?
A: Google Gemini told the group to pack far less food and water than they actually needed for the trip.

Q: Who eventually saved the stranded hikers?
A: Forest Service rangers and local volunteers found the hikers and rescued them the morning after they got lost.
