Y Combinator Boss Garry Tan Wants US Tech Firms to Distill AI Models

A major debate is heating up in Silicon Valley over how artificial intelligence is built. Garry Tan, the head of the famous startup accelerator Y Combinator, recently shared his views on how smaller companies should compete. He wants smaller American AI companies to use a training method called distillation on the world's most powerful AI systems.

This comes at a time when some big AI makers are trying to lock down their technology. Distillation is a process where a smaller AI model learns how to think by asking a larger, smarter model millions of questions. While some big tech firms want to ban this practice, Tan believes it is the key to keeping the AI industry fair and open.

What is Distillation

In simple terms, distillation is like a student learning from a teacher. A smaller, cheaper AI model sends thousands of prompts to a giant, expensive frontier model to see how it answers. By studying these responses, the smaller model can quickly copy the reasoning skills of the larger one. While some big AI companies call this an attack, smaller developers use it as a standard and fast way to build helpful software.

The Battle Over AI Secrets

The debate started after Anthropic, a major AI developer, released a report in 2024. The report accused Chinese labs of using fake accounts and stolen passwords to distill Anthropic's models without permission. The boss of Anthropic wants the US government to step in and stop people from copying models this way. However, Garry Tan argues that big AI companies already built their own systems by scraping massive amounts of copyrighted human data from the internet without asking. He believes those same companies should not be allowed to lock up their AI answers behind strict terms of service.

Why This Matters for the Future

Tan warns that if the government bans distillation, it could lead to a scary future where only one giant corporation controls all advanced AI. By allowing smaller US companies to legally distill big models, we get more open-weight options. These are AI models where the inner workings are shared freely with the public. This gives regular people and smaller startups more freedom to build their own tools without relying on a single tech monopoly.

Quick Questions Answered

What is the difference between closed and open-weight AI?

A: Closed AI is kept secret by the company that made it, while open-weight AI lets developers see and modify the inner settings of the model.

Why does Garry Tan support AI distillation?

A: He believes it helps smaller US startups build better tools quickly, which prevents a single giant tech company from holding a monopoly over all AI.

Did big AI companies use copyrighted material?

A: Yes, major AI developers trained their systems using vast amounts of public writing and art from the internet, often without the creators' permission.

Robot training data startup Mecka nears 500m valuation in major new funding deal

How Mecka Teaches Robots

Instead of using computer simulations, Mecka gathers real-world information by paying regular people to record themselves doing everyday chores. People wear special body sensors and use their smartphones to film themselves doing things like brewing a cup of coffee or repairing a vehicle. This raw human movement information is then used to train AI models, helping future humanoid robots move more naturally and perform physical tasks safely in our homes and workplaces.

Why This Data Is So Valuable

Building smart robots requires a huge amount of physical-world information, which is currently very hard to find. While AI programs like chatbots can learn from billions of words on the internet, robots need to understand how the physical world works. Many tech experts believe this lack of real-world movement information is the main bottleneck stopping us from having useful robotic helpers in our daily lives. This high demand is why Mecka expects its annual revenue rate to reach $100 million by the end of 2026.

The Growing Robot AI Market

Mecka is not the only company trying to solve this problem. Other startups are also raising massive amounts of cash to collect physical information for machines. For example, a rival company called XDOF was recently valued at $1.2 billion. As tech giants and research labs rush to build the earliest truly useful humanoid robots, the race to gather human movement information is quickly becoming among the hottest areas in the tech industry.

How the New iPhone and Samsung Galaxy Compete for the Best Giant Phone

At first glance, these 2 massive devices look quite similar. They both feature huge 6.9-inch screens and classic rectangular designs. However, when you look closer, there are some very important differences in how they feel, how bright their screens get, and how their cameras take photos.

Size, Weight, and Screen Differences

The Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra is surprisingly lighter and thinner than Apple's giant phone. The Galaxy weighs 214 grams and is 7.9 mm thick, while the iPhone 18 Pro Max weighs 249 grams and is 8.75 mm thick. A lighter phone is much easier to hold for long periods. On the other hand, the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra boasts a massive 200-megapixel main camera, which lets you crop into photos without losing detail.

Choosing the Right Giant Phone

Ultimately, your choice will likely come down to which software system you already use. If you love the freedom of Android and want a lighter phone with a built-in stylus, the Galaxy is the clear winner. But if you prefer Apple's iOS system, want a brighter screen for outdoor use, and love the idea of professional-style camera control, the iPhone is a fantastic option.

Is 256gb Enough for an iPhone? Plus, the Power Tool You Should Never Use on Concrete

Getting the right tool for the job is the best way to avoid frustrating headaches. If you are shopping for a new smartphone, you might be wondering if a 256gb iPhone is enough to hold all your files and photos. Making the wrong choice means you might have to delete your favorite memories just to install a basic software update.

At the same time, choosing the wrong equipment for home DIY projects can be just as painful. Using the wrong power tool on tough surfaces like concrete can ruin your gear and even cause injuries. Here is how to make the smart choice for both your digital life and your next home project.

Choosing Your iPhone Storage Size

A starting size of 256GB is plenty of space for most average users. A standard phone photo is only about 3MB, meaning you can store around 70,000 pictures before running out of space. However, files get much larger if you shoot high-quality video or play heavy games. One minute of basic 4K video takes up 440MB of space, and professional ProRes video can eat up 6GB in just one minute. High-resolution RAW photos can also take up 50MB each. Modern games like Resident Evil 4 require more than 30GB of space, and the phone's built-in operating system takes up about 24GB right away. If you do not want to rely on iCloud storage or if you travel frequently without internet, upgrading to 512GB is a smart move.

Drilling Concrete the Right Way

When it comes to home improvement, you should never use a standard drill without hammer action on concrete. Regular drills only spin, which creates intense heat and can quickly ruin your drill bits. Pushing harder on a spinning drill will only make the bit overheat faster, creating a safety hazard for you. Instead, you should use a hammer drill or a rotary percussive hammer drill, which chips away at the stone while spinning. Different jobs require different tools depending on the concrete type, such as lightweight concrete made with pumice, reinforced concrete with steel bars, or high-strength concrete rated at 6,000 PSI or more. Make sure to check your owner's manual and use carbide-tipped bits for masonry work, while saving twist bits for wood and metal.

Quick Questions Answered

How many photos can a 256GB iPhone hold?

A 256GB phone can hold around 70,000 standard photos, since an average picture takes up about 3MB of space.

Why is a regular drill bad for concrete?

A regular drill only rotates and lacks the punching power to break concrete, which causes the drill bit to overheat and break.

What kind of drill bit should I use for concrete?

You should always use carbide-tipped drill bits that are specifically rated for concrete and masonry.

If you open your iPhone today in the US, you might notice a big change on your map. Apple has officially changed the name of Lake Ontario to Lake America on its Apple Maps service. This decision follows a recent order from the US government to rename the famous body of water.

Apple is not the first tech giant to make this update. Google also changed the name on Google Maps last week. Only users inside the US will see the new name. People using the app in Canada will still see Lake Ontario, while users in other countries will see both names on their screens.

Why the Name Changed

The change is a direct result of an executive order signed by President Trump on August 27. This order gave US government departments 30 days to update their digital databases, which are the official electronic filing systems for geographic names. Since Google and Apple get their map information from these official government sources, they updated their apps to match. The two companies made a similar change last year when they renamed the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America.

MapQuest Sees a Huge Boost

Not every mapping service is following the new government rule. MapQuest announced that it will keep using the traditional name of Lake Ontario. Because of this stand, MapQuest has seen a massive surge in popularity. The classic map app quickly became the number 1 free download on the Apple App Store, which is the marketplace where phone users get their apps. It even beat ChatGPT in download numbers on September 1.

A: Apple changed the name to match official US government databases after an executive order was signed on August 27.

Does this name change affect maps in Canada?

A: No, people using map apps in Canada will still see the traditional name of Lake Ontario.

Which mapping apps are keeping the old name?

A: MapQuest has refused to change the name and will continue to show Lake Ontario on its maps.

AI startup AfterQuery reportedly becomes fastest unicorn in history

An artificial intelligence startup called AfterQuery has achieved a massive milestone. The company reportedly reached a valuation of $3.2 billion. This makes it the fastest-ever startup to reach a $1 billion valuation, which is known as a "unicorn" in the business world, in the history of the famous startup incubator Y Combinator.

The incredibly fast rise of AfterQuery has shocked the tech industry. Just 5 months ago, the company was valued at $300 million. Now, its value has multiplied by more than 10 times in less than half a year.

What AfterQuery Does

Most AI training companies focus on making sure chatbots give correct answers. AfterQuery does something different. They hire real-world professionals like doctors and lawyers to train AI models. Instead of just correcting facts, these experts teach the AI how to think and solve problems like a human professional. This helps AI software handle complex tasks by copying the decision-making patterns of top experts.

Young Founders and Huge Revenue

The founders of AfterQuery are only 22 and 23 years old. They participated in the Y Combinator startup program, which helps new companies get started, just 18 months ago. Since then, their business has grown at a blistering speed. By April, the startup was already on track to make $100 million in yearly revenue. They now work with major tech giants, including the famous computer chipmaker Nvidia.

The Rise of AI Training

Training AI models has become one of the most lucrative areas in technology today. Tech companies are spending billions of dollars to make their systems smarter and more useful. Companies like AfterQuery are in high demand because they provide the high-quality human data needed to build the next generation of superintelligent software.

FTC accuses Amazon in new lawsuit over a secret ad surcharge scheme

The US government and 22 states have filed a major lawsuit against Amazon. The Federal Trade Commission, or FTC, accuses Amazon of running a secret ad surcharge scheme. Regulators claim this trick helped the company quietly pocket billions of dollars by overcharging businesses that advertise on its website.

The lawsuit alleges this practice went on for more than 7 years. It reportedly affected over 1 million brands and sellers who use Amazon to reach shoppers.

How the Ad Auctions Worked

The lawsuit focuses on how Amazon sells its search result ads, like Sponsored Products. Normally, Amazon told sellers it used a second-price auction. In this system, the winner of the ad spot only pays 1 cent more than the next highest bid. This gave businesses the confidence to bid high, believing they would not actually have to pay their maximum bid. However, the FTC claims that starting in 2019, Amazon secretly changed the rules. They allegedly used fake bidders and hidden price floors to force advertisers to pay their full maximum bids nearly 80% of the time.

Why Amazon Did It

According to the lawsuit, Amazon made this change simply to increase its own advertising revenue. The company feared that if they told advertisers about the price hikes, those businesses would lower their bids. This secret change reportedly helped boost Amazon's massive advertising business, which brought in more than $68 billion in 2023.

Amazon's Response

Amazon strongly denies these claims. The company released a blog post calling the lawsuit misguided. It stated that the FTC does not understand how advertising works. Amazon says that ad prices naturally change because their system looks at billions of bids at once, and they keep advertisers properly informed.

Quick Questions Answered

Who is suing Amazon over these ad fees?

A: The Federal Trade Commission and 22 US states are suing the company.

How long did this alleged scheme last?

A: Regulators say Amazon ran this hidden ad pricing system for more than 7 years.

What does Amazon say about the lawsuit?

A: Amazon claims the lawsuit is misguided and misinterprets how its auction system works.
