What is a Variable Aperture Camera?

In photography, the aperture is the physical opening in a lens that lets light reach the camera sensor. Standard smartphone cameras have a fixed opening size that never changes. A variable aperture camera allows the lens to physically open wider or close down narrower depending on the situation. This means you can take much better photos in dark rooms and have more control over how blurry the background looks in your portrait shots.

How DeepMind Alumni Are Building Tools To Accelerate Fusion Power

A group of 2 former scientists from Google's artificial intelligence division, DeepMind, have started a new company to help make clean energy a reality. These DeepMind alumni are building tools to accelerate fusion power so we can eventually use it to light up our homes. Their new startup, called Fusionality, wants to solve one of the biggest engineering headaches in the clean energy world.

Fusion power is a clean way of making electricity by joining atoms together, which is exactly how the sun creates energy. To do this on Earth, scientists have to control a super-hot gas called plasma. This gas is incredibly hot and difficult to manage, requiring split-second decisions to keep it stable. Fusionality is creating the computerized control systems, or the brains, to manage these wild reactions automatically.

The Problem They Are Solving

Right now, many different companies are trying to build their own fusion reactors. However, almost all of these companies are building their control systems completely from scratch. The founders of Fusionality realized that about 80% of the control technology is exactly the same for every reactor design. Instead of every company reinventing the wheel, Fusionality will sell ready-to-use control tools and computer simulations that any fusion company can customize.

Cash to Build the Future

The startup is based in Switzerland and has already raised $3.7 million in early funding. The company currently has 7 employees. They plan to use this money to build their first set of control technologies, which they compare to building blocks. Initially, they are focusing on reactors that use powerful magnets to hold the hot gas in place, but they plan to expand to other types of reactors later.

The Role of Artificial Intelligence

Because the founders have a background in artificial intelligence, many people wonder if AI will run these reactors. The founders say that AI will certainly help optimize and improve the control systems. However, they do not think AI should completely run a fusion reactor on its own yet. Instead, AI will work alongside traditional computer programs to keep things running safely.

What is Fusionality?

It is a new startup founded by former Google DeepMind researchers that builds control systems and software tools for fusion power reactors.

How much money did the startup raise?

Fusionality raised $3.7 million in its pre-seed funding round to help build its technology.

What is fusion power?

It is a clean energy source created by merging atoms together under extreme heat, mimicking the way the sun makes energy.

Hackers Are Stealing Claude Tokens From Paid Subscribers

Imagine paying for a premium service, only to find someone else is secretly using it all up. That is exactly what is happening to some people who pay for Claude, a popular artificial intelligence tool. Cybercriminals are finding ways to hijack user accounts and drain their monthly allowances.

These hackers are stealing Claude tokens from paid subscribers by using sneaky software to grab login details. Tokens are basically the digital credits you use every time you ask the AI to write, code, or search the web. When these tokens get stolen, paying users are left locked out of the tool they need for their daily work.

How the Theft Happens

Security teams at Anthropic, the company that makes Claude, found that hackers are using infostealer malware. This is a type of malicious software that slips onto your computer when you download sketchy files or click on bad ads. Once inside, it quietly copies your saved passwords and active login sessions. The hackers then use these stolen sessions to log into Claude as if they were you, burning through your paid limits to run their own tasks.

The Impact on Users

One independent consultant in the UK noticed his account usage kept rising even when his computer was turned off. He pays $200 every month for a high-end Claude plan to help run his business. After he complained, Anthropic suspended his account to stop the theft and gave him a partial refund of £44.49. However, losing access to the AI tool temporarily disrupted his entire business. Other users on Reddit and GitHub have reported similar issues, with their usage limits jumping from 0 to 100 percent in minutes.

How to Protect Yourself

Anthropic has been warning affected users and signing them out of all active sessions to stop the theft. To keep your account safe, you should run a virus scan on your computer to check for malware. Avoid downloading software from untrustworthy websites and do not click on suspicious links or online ads. Since Anthropic does not currently provide an itemized list of your daily usage, keeping a close eye on your account's usage meter is the best way to spot anything unusual.

Quick Questions Answered

What is a Claude token?

A token is a unit of data that Claude uses to process your requests. Every time you ask the AI a question or have it write code, you use up a certain number of tokens from your monthly allowance.

Is the Claude website itself unsafe?

No, the malware does not come from the Claude website itself. Hackers infect your computer through other sources, like malicious downloads or bad ads, and then steal your saved login data.

What should I do if my Claude usage suddenly spikes?

You should contact Anthropic support immediately to suspend active sessions. You should also run an antivirus scan on your computer to find and remove any hidden malware.

What is opaque recurrence? The new AI terms you should probably know

Artificial intelligence is changing our world at a rapid pace. Along with this tech boom comes a wave of confusing new jargon that can make anyone feel left behind. Last week, safety researchers started worrying about a concept called opaque recurrence, which is a new reasoning technique used in OpenAI's latest Astra model.

To help you stay on top of these conversations, we have broken down some of the most common AI terms you should probably know. You do not need a computer science degree to understand how these new tools are shaping our future.

What is opaque recurrence and reasoning?

Standard AI chatbots usually give you an answer instantly without showing how they got there. Newer models use what is called chain-of-thought reasoning, where the computer breaks a big problem down into smaller, logical steps before answering. However, with opaque recurrence, the AI does this extra thinking hidden deep inside its own network. Because humans cannot easily see or monitor this internal thinking process, safety experts are worried about what the AI might be deciding behind closed doors.

Understanding AGI and AI agents

You might also hear people talk about AGI, which stands for artificial general intelligence. This refers to a future AI that can perform most intellectual tasks better than a human can. While AGI does not exist yet, we are starting to see the rise of AI agents. Unlike simple chatbots that only talk to you, an AI agent is a tool designed to actually perform multi-step chores on your behalf, such as booking a flight or filing your monthly expenses.

How software talks to itself

For these smart agents to work, they need to connect with other programs. They do this using API endpoints, which you can think of as digital buttons built into software. While human users usually do not see these buttons, AI tools can press them to share data or complete tasks automatically. This allows different apps to work together seamlessly without you having to click a single thing.

Quick Questions Answered

What does AGI stand for?

A: AGI stands for artificial general intelligence, which is a theoretical AI that can perform most cognitive tasks as well as or better than a human.

Why are researchers worried about opaque recurrence?

A: Researchers are concerned because the AI processes its steps and reasoning internally, making it very difficult for humans to monitor or understand how it reached its final conclusion.

What is the difference between a chatbot and an AI agent?

A: A chatbot is designed to hold conversations and answer questions, while an AI agent can actively perform tasks for you across different apps, like booking reservations.

Is 256gb Enough for an iPhone? Plus, the Power Tool You Should Never Use on Concrete

Getting the right tool for the job is the best way to avoid frustrating headaches. If you are shopping for a new smartphone, you might be wondering if a 256gb iPhone is enough to hold all your files and photos. Making the wrong choice means you might have to delete your favorite memories just to install a basic software update.

At the same time, choosing the wrong equipment for home DIY projects can be just as painful. Using the wrong power tool on tough surfaces like concrete can ruin your gear and even cause injuries. Here is how to make the smart choice for both your digital life and your next home project.

Choosing Your iPhone Storage Size

A starting size of 256GB is plenty of space for most average users. A standard phone photo is only about 3MB, meaning you can store around 70,000 pictures before running out of space. However, files get much larger if you shoot high-quality video or play heavy games. One minute of basic 4K video takes up 440MB of space, and professional ProRes video can eat up 6GB in just one minute. High-resolution RAW photos can also take up 50MB each. Modern games like Resident Evil 4 require more than 30GB of space, and the phone's built-in operating system takes up about 24GB right away. If you do not want to rely on iCloud storage or if you travel frequently without internet, upgrading to 512GB is a smart move.

Drilling Concrete the Right Way

When it comes to home improvement, you should never use a standard drill without hammer action on concrete. Regular drills only spin, which creates intense heat and can quickly ruin your drill bits. Pushing harder on a spinning drill will only make the bit overheat faster, creating a safety hazard for you. Instead, you should use a hammer drill or a rotary percussive hammer drill, which chips away at the stone while spinning. Different jobs require different tools depending on the concrete type, such as lightweight concrete made with pumice, reinforced concrete with steel bars, or high-strength concrete rated at 6,000 PSI or more. Make sure to check your owner's manual and use carbide-tipped bits for masonry work, while saving twist bits for wood and metal.

Why is a regular drill bad for concrete?

A regular drill only rotates and lacks the punching power to break concrete, which causes the drill bit to overheat and break.

What kind of drill bit should I use for concrete?

You should always use carbide-tipped drill bits that are specifically rated for concrete and masonry.

FTC accuses Amazon in new lawsuit over a secret ad surcharge scheme

The US government and 22 states have filed a major lawsuit against Amazon. The Federal Trade Commission, or FTC, accuses Amazon of running a secret ad surcharge scheme. Regulators claim this trick helped the company quietly pocket billions of dollars by overcharging businesses that advertise on its website.

The lawsuit alleges this practice went on for more than 7 years. It reportedly affected over 1 million brands and sellers who use Amazon to reach shoppers.

How the Ad Auctions Worked

The lawsuit focuses on how Amazon sells its search result ads, like Sponsored Products. Normally, Amazon told sellers it used a second-price auction. In this system, the winner of the ad spot only pays 1 cent more than the next highest bid. This gave businesses the confidence to bid high, believing they would not actually have to pay their maximum bid. However, the FTC claims that starting in 2019, Amazon secretly changed the rules. They allegedly used fake bidders and hidden price floors to force advertisers to pay their full maximum bids nearly 80% of the time.

Why Amazon Did It

According to the lawsuit, Amazon made this change simply to increase its own advertising revenue. The company feared that if they told advertisers about the price hikes, those businesses would lower their bids. This secret change reportedly helped boost Amazon's massive advertising business, which brought in more than $68 billion in 2023.

Amazon's Response

Amazon strongly denies these claims. The company released a blog post calling the lawsuit misguided. It stated that the FTC does not understand how advertising works. Amazon says that ad prices naturally change because their system looks at billions of bids at once, and they keep advertisers properly informed.

A: The Federal Trade Commission and 22 US states are suing the company.

How long did this alleged scheme last?

A: Regulators say Amazon ran this hidden ad pricing system for more than 7 years.

A: Amazon claims the lawsuit is misguided and misinterprets how its auction system works.
