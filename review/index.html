Dyson Launches $500 CameraJet Toothbrush That Scans Your Mouth

The famous vacuum cleaner company Dyson is trying something completely new. They just announced the Dyson CameraJet, a smart toothbrush that scans your mouth to brush, floss, and rinse your teeth all at the same time.

This new device costs $500 and uses a built-in camera and artificial intelligence to find the gaps between your teeth. When the brush spots a gap, it shoots a tiny jet of water or mouthwash to clean it out instantly. This means you can brush and floss your teeth in one easy step.

How the Smart Tech Works

The toothbrush has a small camera mounted right under the brush head. This camera takes 28 pictures every second. A smart computer system, which was trained on 470,000 dental images, reads these pictures in real time. Within 100 milliseconds of seeing a gap between your teeth, the brush fires a quick, targeted burst of water. The brush holds about 2.5 teaspoons of water in a special tank that works even if you hold the toothbrush upside down.

Using the Companion App

You can connect the toothbrush to the MyDyson app on your phone. This app shows you a live video of the inside of your mouth so you can see hard-to-reach areas behind your teeth. Dyson says that none of your video data is recorded or saved in the cloud. The app also tracks how well you brush and gives you tips on how to improve.

Price and Release Details

The Dyson CameraJet will cost $500. While Dyson has officially revealed the device, they have not yet announced the exact date when it will go on sale. It represents a major upgrade compared to standard electric toothbrushes because it combines a camera, a water flosser, and a brush into one handheld tool.

Quick Questions Answered

How much does the Dyson CameraJet cost?

The Dyson CameraJet costs $500.

Does the toothbrush save video recordings of your mouth?

No, the MyDyson app only shows a live view and does not store any images or video on your device or in the cloud.

How does the flossing feature work?

A camera scans your teeth 28 times per second and uses AI to find gaps, then shoots tiny jets of water to clean them.

FTC accuses Amazon in new lawsuit over a secret ad surcharge scheme

The US government and 22 states have filed a major lawsuit against Amazon. The Federal Trade Commission, or FTC, accuses Amazon of running a secret ad surcharge scheme. Regulators claim this trick helped the company quietly pocket billions of dollars by overcharging businesses that advertise on its website.

The lawsuit alleges this practice went on for more than 7 years. It reportedly affected over 1 million brands and sellers who use Amazon to reach shoppers.

How the Ad Auctions Worked

The lawsuit focuses on how Amazon sells its search result ads, like Sponsored Products. Normally, Amazon told sellers it used a second-price auction. In this system, the winner of the ad spot only pays 1 cent more than the next highest bid. This gave businesses the confidence to bid high, believing they would not actually have to pay their maximum bid. However, the FTC claims that starting in 2019, Amazon secretly changed the rules. They allegedly used fake bidders and hidden price floors to force advertisers to pay their full maximum bids nearly 80% of the time.

Why Amazon Did It

According to the lawsuit, Amazon made this change simply to increase its own advertising revenue. The company feared that if they told advertisers about the price hikes, those businesses would lower their bids. This secret change reportedly helped boost Amazon's massive advertising business, which brought in more than $68 billion in 2023.

Amazon's Response

Amazon strongly denies these claims. The company released a blog post calling the lawsuit misguided. It stated that the FTC does not understand how advertising works. Amazon says that ad prices naturally change because their system looks at billions of bids at once, and they keep advertisers properly informed.

Quick Questions Answered

Who is suing Amazon over these ad fees?

A: The Federal Trade Commission and 22 US states are suing the company.

How long did this alleged scheme last?

A: Regulators say Amazon ran this hidden ad pricing system for more than 7 years.

What does Amazon say about the lawsuit?

A: Amazon claims the lawsuit is misguided and misinterprets how its auction system works.

Why the US is building barriers around foreign robots and drones

The US government is putting up strict new rules and taxes on automated machines made abroad. Washington recently restricted advanced robotic systems and placed high taxes on imported drones. Officials say these steps are necessary to protect national security. However, these actions are raising big questions about the future of the technology market.

By building barriers around robots and drones from other countries, the US hopes to protect its own businesses. But China already makes these machines at a massive scale and at very low costs. Because of this, Chinese companies might easily find ways around these restrictions by selling to the rest of the world instead.

The Scale of the Giant

The gap in manufacturing between the US and China is very large. In the first half of 2026, global shipments of humanoid robots—which are robots designed to look and move like humans—reached 22,000 units. Chinese companies made a massive 86% of those machines. Five Chinese firms currently lead the global market. Because they make so many robots, they can lower their prices much faster than American rivals can. This also lets them collect more real-world data to make their machines smarter.

Where the Tech Goes Next

If these affordable machines cannot enter the US, they will simply go elsewhere. Experts say Chinese brands are already targeting other parts of the world. They are focusing on regions with labor shortages that need cheap automation, such as Europe, Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East. This could create a split global market. The US and its allies might use more expensive, highly secure robots, while the rest of the world adopts cheaper Chinese technology.

Software Versus Hardware

The US still holds a major advantage in creating advanced artificial intelligence, which is the software or "brains" behind these machines. American companies also lead in making the powerful computer chips needed to run them. However, building physical machines requires deep supply chains and factories. China excels at this physical manufacturing. Experts warn that the US cannot just block its way to success and must invest heavily in its own factories to keep up.

Quick Questions Answered

Why is the US blocking foreign drones and robots?

A: The US government has raised national security concerns about foreign-made technology spying on citizens or disrupting critical systems.

Will these new rules stop China from making robots?

A: No, because Chinese companies can easily sell their low-cost machines to other growing markets in Europe, Asia, and Latin America.

How much of the humanoid robot market does China control?

A: Chinese manufacturers made 86% of all humanoid robots shipped globally in the first half of 2026.

Google Pixel 11 Pro Review: Excellent Cameras but a Weird Feature is Finally Dead

The new Google Pixel 11 Pro has arrived, and it brings some major updates along with one surprising departure. If you are looking at Google's latest flagship phones, you might notice that a very unusual piece of hardware has vanished. Google has officially removed the infrared temperature sensor that first showed up on Pro models a few years ago.

In this Google Pixel 11 Pro review, we will look at how this new phone stacks up against the standard Pixel 11, what is replacing that quirky sensor, and whether the upgrade is worth your money. While some people laughed at the built-in thermometer when it first launched, many users grew to love it for checking everything from fevers to how hot the pavement was before walking their dogs. Now, that era is over.

What is New and What is Gone

Instead of the temperature sensor, the Pixel 11 Pro features a new ring of lights around the camera flash called HiLight. This light glows in different colors to show you notifications or to let you know when the Gemini artificial intelligence assistant is listening to you. Inside the phone, the Pro model also shines with a brighter screen that uses special LTPO technology to change its refresh rate automatically, which saves battery power. You also get up to 16GB of RAM, which is short for Random Access Memory and helps the phone run multiple apps at the same time, though the basic Pixel 11 is still very fast in daily use.

The Camera Upgrades

The camera is the biggest reason to choose the Pro version over the standard Pixel 11. Although both models share a 3-lens zoom system, the Pixel 11 Pro uses much better sensors for every single camera. It has a 50-megapixel main lens, a 48-megapixel telephoto lens for zooming, a 48-megapixel ultrawide lens for wide landscape shots, and a powerful 42-megapixel front camera for selfies. These high-resolution sensors work with Google's smart photo software to take incredibly detailed photos, especially in low light.

Price and What is in the Box

You will have to pay a bit more for these extra features, as the Pro model costs about $200 more than the regular Pixel 11. Specifically, the version with 256GB of storage and 12GB of RAM starts at $1,098.82 in the US, which is £1,079.00 in the UK. If you want the top-tier 512GB version with 16GB of RAM, it will cost you $1,219.00 or £1,199.00. Keep in mind that Google only includes a USB-C cable in the box, so you will need to buy your own charger and a case, which now supports magnetic accessories.

Quick Questions Answered

Does the Pixel 11 Pro have a temperature sensor?

A: No, Google has completely removed the infrared thermometer from the Pixel 11 series and replaced it with a notification light ring called HiLight.

How much does the Pixel 11 Pro cost?

A: The phone starts at $1,098.82 or £1,079.00 for the 256GB model, while the 512GB model costs $1,219.00 or £1,199.00.

What accessories come in the box with the Pixel 11 Pro?

A: The phone only comes with a USB-C cable, so you must buy your own wall charger and protective case separately.

Samsung Galaxy A35 and A56 Could Receive One UI 9 Beta Soon

Samsung is currently working on its next major software update for its phones. This update is called One UI 9, and it is based on Google's Android 17 operating system. While only a few of Samsung's most expensive phones have access to this new software right now, two of its popular mid-range phones might get a sneak peek very soon.

Tech experts have spotted test software for the Samsung Galaxy A35 and the Samsung Galaxy A56 on Samsung's private servers. This suggests that these two affordable models could receive the One UI 9 beta build before the final version rolls out. A beta is a test version of software that lets users try new features early and help find bugs before the official release.

Why This Is Great News for Budget Phone Owners

Samsung usually releases its test software only to its most expensive, top-tier phones first. Mid-range phone owners often have to wait many months for the final, stable version without getting to test it early. A stable version is the official, finished software that is safe for everyone to use. If the Galaxy A35 and Galaxy A56 get this test version, it means owners of these budget-friendly phones can try the new Android 17 features much earlier than usual.

What We Know About the Rollout

The evidence comes from Samsung's own test servers in Korea, where special software files for both phones were recently spotted. Samsung has also set up an official community forum section for the Galaxy A56 test program in India. Right now, only a few of Samsung's newest devices, like the Galaxy Z Fold8 and the Galaxy S26 FE, run the finished One UI 9 software because they launched with it. Meanwhile, the flagship Galaxy S26 series is currently on its 7th test version and should get the final update in the coming weeks, with older phones like the Galaxy S25 following shortly after.

Quick Questions Answered

What is One UI 9?

A: It is Samsung's upcoming software update for its smartphones, built on top of the Android 17 operating system.

Which budget Samsung phones might get the test update?

A: The Galaxy A35 and the Galaxy A56 are the two mid-range phones that have been spotted with test files on Samsung's servers.

What is the difference between a beta and a stable build?

A: A beta is an early test version of software that might have bugs, while a stable build is the final, finished version released to the public.
