Judge rules Pentagon ban on AI giant Anthropic is illegal

A US federal judge rules that the Pentagon acted against the law when it banned the artificial intelligence company Anthropic. The court decided that labeling the tech firm as a security risk was illegal and had no actual basis. This ruling stops several government agencies from enforcing an executive order by President Donald Trump to block the company's software.

This is a major win for Anthropic, which makes the popular Claude AI tool. The judge made it clear that the government cannot use national security claims as an excuse to punish companies that criticize its policies.

Why the Government Banned the AI

The disagreement started in February when government officials pushed Anthropic to remove safety guardrails from its AI systems. Anthropic's boss, Dario Amodei, refused to let the company's AI be used for mass spying or building robotic weapons. In response, President Trump ordered government agencies to stop using the software within 6 months, and the defense department labeled the startup as a supply chain risk.

What Happens Next for Anthropic

Anthropic sued the White House in two separate courts to fight the ban. While this court decision is a big victory, the company still has to win its second lawsuit before the security risk label is fully removed. Even if Anthropic wins both cases, the Pentagon does not have to start buying its services again. However, the legal battle has not hurt the company's business, as its revenues recently jumped to $11.5 billion ahead of a planned public stock launch later in 2025.

Quick Questions Answered

Why did the US government ban Anthropic's AI?

The government tried to ban the company after Anthropic refused to let its AI be used for mass surveillance or autonomous weapons.

What did the federal judge decide about the ban?

The judge ruled that the defense department's ban was illegal and baseless, and stopped federal agencies from enforcing it.

Is Anthropic completely cleared to work with the government now?

Not yet, because Anthropic filed two lawsuits and still needs to win the second case to fully clear its name, though the Pentagon is not forced to buy its products.

Bluesky adds a new algorithmic opt-out feature to stop your posts going viral

The popular social media platform Bluesky adds a new algorithmic opt-out feature for users who want to share their thoughts without drawing massive crowds. This handy tool lets you block your posts from appearing in the app’s main Discover feed, where anyone on the network can normally see them.

This new update comes right after the platform started letting users upload longer videos. It is a welcome option for people who just want to talk to their existing friends and followers, rather than being pushed into the spotlight by computer formulas.

How the New Setting Works

To use this feature, you just need to open your Privacy and Security settings in the app and turn on the new option. The company says it can take up to 1 hour for the change to take effect across the network. Because this choice is saved directly to your account, it will even apply if you use other apps connected to the same network system.

Public But Less Visible

It is important to remember that this update does not make your posts private. Your profile is still public, and people can still see your posts if they visit your page directly. Bluesky is currently working on a way to offer truly private accounts in the future, but this tool is simply meant to keep your posts from being recommended to strangers.

Quick Questions Answered

How do I stop my Bluesky posts from going viral?

A: You can go to your Privacy and Security settings in the app and turn on the new opt-out option to keep your posts off the main feed.

Does this new option make my account private?

A: No, your account remains public, but your posts will no longer be pushed out to the wider public through recommendations.

How fast does the change happen?

A: It can take up to 1 hour for the system to stop showing your posts in the feed after you turn the setting on.
